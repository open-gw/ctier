# 51 — schemas contradict a rule nobody named

Spec **1.21.0**. Requires 43, 48.
Independent of the engine. Engine
behaviour was not changed. No
criterion reworded. CITATION.cff
remains 0.1.0.

43 found the orphan MUST:
consumers ignore fields they do
not recognise. It left it unnamed.
41 wrote `declaredTier: 4` because
Attempt required an integer. 50
put `epoch` and `seq` beside the
JSON because the record schema
was closed. Both hit the constraint
the unnamed rule contradicts.

A requirement with no identifier
cannot be referenced, cannot be
applied, cannot be noticed when
contradicted. This pass names it
first. Available because 43 wrote
the orphan down instead of fixing
quietly.

---

## Part 1 — the identifier

**Additive evolution.** Titled in
`spec/validation.md` and at the
close of the version history.
Wording unaltered.

It does not take a letter. The
accounting remains fifteen. The
three remaining 1.17.0 candidates
are unchanged.

The admission rule's scope is
implementation behaviour that
mints a C-series criterion,
testable against a running
deployment or a compiled artefact.
It does not admit a rule about
the specification's own documents.

A consumer of records is a second
implementation's client or auditor.
That is a compiled artefact. The
MUST is testable against it: feed
it a field it does not recognise.
That is not enough. The MUST is
on the reader of a document, not
on the enforcement point. 1.20.0
already drew the line: a header
rule is a header rule. A consumer
of records is a named validation
rule.

Minting C16 would have been a
count change. A count change
breaks downstream iteration.
Tooling that walks C1 through C15
would silently skip it. The
orphan was never among the five
1.17.0 candidates. Those are
enforcement-point properties.
This is not.

---

## Part 2 — the contradiction

The rule: tolerate unknown fields.
The ledger schemas:
`additionalProperties: false`.
An unrecognised field is invalid.
Both cannot hold of the same
object. Stated in the document,
not only resolved.

Three options.

Open the schemas. Additive works.
A misspelling becomes valid. This
project already shipped a
wrong-fact record (`declaredTier:
4` for unknown). Opening would
have made the next misspelling a
valid record too.

Keep closed, withdraw the rule.
Versioning is how the format
evolves. Withdrawing a named
requirement because the schemas
were closed is the engine minting
the format. Closed schema is a
mechanism for typo-catching.
Additive evolution is a property.
Mechanism-for-property is the
standing enemy.

Keep closed, scope the rule.
Chosen. The MUST is unaltered.
Its scope is unknown `x-ctier-*`
headers at the trust boundary,
and unrecognised keys in a named
extensions object where a
document type defines one. It
does not apply to the closed
property set of a ledger record.
Adding a field to a closed record
is a new record-type version.

The original paragraph already
named the header invariant. The
scope is that invariant, and the
named bag a document type may
define, not a compromise sentence
that lets both hold of the
record.

---

## Part 3 — declaredTier

Present `declaredTier` is the
declaration that was made.
Absent is the declaration that
was not. Different facts. 0.2.0
collapsed them into one integer.

`attempt-record` moves to `0.3.0`.
`declaredTier` is no longer
required. Type remains integer
1–4 when present. Do not write 4
to mean unknown.

`decision-record` stays `0.2.0`.
The field was already optional.
It also permits `null`. Absence
is the specified expression of
unknown.

A reader who sees no
`declaredTier` may conclude that
no declaration was made. A reader
who sees an integer may conclude
that that declaration was made.
A reader who sees `4` may not
conclude unknown. 4 is Tier 4.

---

## Part 4 — sequence

An auditable record does not
carry its own sequence.

1.19.0 put absence-detectable on
the sink. C10's third sink clause
is a property of the sink, not of
the record object. 50 put `epoch`
and `seq` on the log line, outside
the JSON, because the schema was
closed.

That arrangement is
conformant-by-statement. The
sink is auditable. The record is
evidence a decision was made. A
consumer that extracts the JSON
and discards the line envelope
has left the sink. Gap detection
was never a property of the
object they kept.

Not a named gap. The closed
schemas do not grow these fields.

---

## Part 5 — versioning

Specification **1.21.0**, not
2.0.0.

Record types are pre-1.0 and may
break. 1.4.0 already moved
`0.1.0` to `0.2.0` as a spec
minor. `attempt-record/0.3.0` is
that class of bump. It says what
breaks: a 0.2.0 validator rejects
a 0.3.0 Attempt that omits
`declaredTier`; writers emit
`attempt-record/0.3.0`. That
breakage is named on the record
type.

Scoping Additive evolution
states where the rule already
held. It does not withdraw a
working contract on closed
records. That contract never
held: the schemas rejected the
field. A major would treat the
contradiction as if both had
been in force.

The C-count is unchanged.
CITATION.cff stays 0.1.0. A
spec 2.0.0 for a scoped
validation rule and a pre-1.0
record bump would be the count
change that does not say what
stops iterating: everything that
pins 1.x would look skipped.

## What the engine now has to do

Report only. This repository
does not edit the engine.

The engine vendors schemas
**1.4.0** (`attempt-record/0.2.0`,
`decision-record/0.2.0`). It pins
specification **1.15.0**. It
writes `declaredTier: 4` on an
unassigned Attempt. It puts
`epoch` and `seq` on the
error-log line.

It has to:

- Vendor `attempt-record/0.3.0`
  and emit `ctierSchema:
  attempt-record/0.3.0`.
- Omit `declaredTier` when no
  declaration was made. Stop
  writing 4 for unknown.
- Keep `epoch` and `seq` on the
  sink line. That is now
  conformant-by-statement, not a
  workaround for a closed schema.
- Not claim 1.21.0 while the pin
  is 1.15.0. Claiming 1.21.0
  also claims C14, C15, and
  `governed-set` as a titled
  identifier. 50 already named
  that tension.

A 0.2.0 Attempt that invented
`declaredTier: 4` remains a
0.2.0 record. It is not 0.3.0.

## The observation this pass earns

The cost of an orphan, measured.
43 found the MUST and left it
unnamed. 41 and 50 then ran into
the constraint it contradicts,
and neither could cite the rule
they were colliding with.

A closed schema catching a typo
is a mechanism. Ignore-unknown
is a property. Without a name,
the mechanism won by default —
twice — and the property could
not be applied or scoped.

Naming first was the work. The
contradiction could be stated
only after the rule had a name
to keep.
