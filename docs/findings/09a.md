# 09a — what implementing C5 taught

C5 was written before anything implemented it. Task 09 built the `202` and
found four things the criterion did not say. Three were missing
requirements. One was a prohibition that matters more than the others
combined: the withheld response MUST NOT carry a polling affordance,
because a `pollUrl` does not violate "non-retryable" by letter and
recreates the occupancy the design exists to prevent.

Those four are now in the specification (1.1.7). This note is about the
shape of the gap, not the contents. C5 is not special. It is the first
criterion something was built against. The useful question is which
others will produce the same class of surprise when task 10 builds
against them.

## The shape

A criterion written before contact names an outcome and omits:

1. **Who constructs the artefact.** C5 named the properties. It did not
   say the policy service builds the body and the enforcement point
   relays it. Two edges compiling one contract is the divergence the
   corpus exists to catch.
2. **A prohibition on the natural addition.** Terminal and non-retryable
   do not, by letter, forbid a status URL. A helpful engineer adds one
   within a week. The specification has to say no before that happens.
3. **Fields that lived only in the example.** `authority` and `expiresAt`
   were on the schema and the solution-design row. They were not in C5.
4. **A security property of what must not be stored.** `expiresAt` needs
   the credential's expiry, not the credential. An implementation that
   stores a token in order to compute a deadline has stored a token.

The last of those is why the identity contrast belongs in the
specification at all. Once the policy service cannot keep the agent's
credential, Tier 3 cannot execute under it. That was implicit in the
model and contradicted in the classification guide, which still says
Tier 3 runs "under the agent's credential." Task 10 will hit that
sentence. The specification now says otherwise.

## C6 has the same shape

C6 says execution is from persisted context, byte-identical to the
operation described in the approval event, and that the agent does not
resubmit. Task 10 builds the execute half against that sentence.

It does not say **who the HTTP client is**. Byte-identical does not, by
letter, forbid presenting the agent's credential on the replay —
forwarding the token is the natural reading of "the same request." The
no-credential-persistence rule now forbids keeping that token, and the
tier table now says Tier 3 executes under the policy service's own
identity. C6 itself still names neither. An implementer reading only
the criterion will mint nothing and try to reuse what was persisted.

It also names two sources in one sentence: persisted context, and the
approval event. Those can diverge. C5's lesson was resolve once, in the
place that knows. C6 needs the same: the bytes that execute are the
bytes that were persisted, and the approval event refers to them. It
does not reconstruct them.

"Byte-identical" cannot mean the HTTP envelope. The `Authorization`
header is rewritten: the agent's credential is gone, the service
identity is presented. The operation (method, path, query, body, the
headers that are the request) is what must not change. Without that
carve-out, service-identity execution looks like a C6 violation.

Same four omissions, one criterion later.

## C8 has the occupancy trap

C8 says rejection pauses the agent for the operation class, and expiry
refuses the single operation and leaves the agent working. C5 now says
the agent's involvement with a withheld operation **ends when it
receives the `202`**. Those two sentences are not yet reconciled.

A pause implemented as a callback, a webhook, or a second body to the
agent that already left is `pollUrl` under another name. Expiry and
rejection are dispositions of the **operation**. They are recorded; they
condition the next call of that class. They are not a reason to re-engage
the caller of the withheld request.

C8 does not name who constructs those outcomes, what the agent-facing
shape is (if any), or that there is none for the request that already
returned. Task 10 should not invent a second agent response in order to
satisfy "pauses the agent." The pause is a property of subsequent
attempts, not a notification.

The classification guide already treats C8 as FAQ prose
("Does a Tier 3 rejection pause the agent?"). That is the same
under-specification, in a place an implementer will read first.

## What this is not

C4 already said the human is never in the request path. The polling
prohibition is C4's dual: the agent is not in the review path either.
C7 (derived idempotency keys) has a milder constructor gap — who
derives — but the derivation inputs are named, so two implementations
are less free to diverge. C9 already says Tier 4 has no resume under
the agent's identity; 1.1.7 now says what identity Tier 3 *does* use.

No NEW-MATTER. Relaying a body, deriving a deadline from an `exp`
value, and executing later under a service identity were the model;
they are now requirements rather than folklore.

## 09a.1 — the version contradicted itself

The identity contrast landed in `spec/extensions.md` while four other
places still had the agent executing. That was a belief, not a typo.
The four:

1. **Classification guide FAQ** ("Isn't Tier 4 just Tier 3 with worse
   consequences?"): "the agent's payload, under the agent's credential."
2. **The same FAQ's attribution sentence:** "the agent placed the order
   and a clinician approved it" — execution attributed to the agent.
3. **Landing-page prose** under the tier table: "the agent's operation
   eventually executes with a named approver against it."
4. **Financial-services T+4m 200ms:** "mints a scoped credential" — the
   execute step as minting something to call as the agent, rather than
   presenting the policy service's own identity.

The domain-example tables and `spec/` itself did not carry it. The FAQ,
the landing page, and the worked trace did: the places written to
explain the model, not to declare keys.

C6, read literally, required storing the agent's credential in order to
replay it, which the same version's `validation.md` forbids. The
specification defeated itself in the direction of storing tokens. C6
now scopes byte-identity to method, target, query and body, and names
the policy service as the client.

C8's pause, read against C5, looked like a missing notification. The
gap is intentional: rejection is observed on the next attempt, not
announced. The effect still requires agent-suspension state at the
authorisation layer. Without that, an implementation may record a
rejection but cannot produce it, and must not ask the policy service
on every request whether the agent is paused.

## C7, C9, C10

C5 and C6 both had gaps that appeared only when something was built
against them. The remaining three have not.

**C7** is the one that still has C6's envelope-scope trap. Keys are
derived from `operationId` (or method + path template) plus a canonical
serialisation of "the request specifics." Read literally, that includes
the `Authorization` header. Hashing a credential into an idempotency
key is not storing the token, but it is mixing a secret into a value
that is persisted, and two tokens for the same operation would produce
two keys. The derivation inputs want the same scope C6 just acquired:
method, target, query, body — not the credential. Who derives (enforcement
point or policy service) is unnamed, which is C5's constructor gap in
milder form.

**C9** says Tier 4 has no resume under the agent's identity. That
survives the identity contrast: Tier 4 still must not execute as the
agent. It does not name who constructs the `423` body, which is the
constructor gap again, not the occupancy trap. A handoff URL on that
response would be a different mistake (inviting the agent to watch the
human) and is not yet forbidden the way C5 forbids `pollUrl`.

**C10** says decisions are recorded before they are acted on. ADR-005
already named the unavailable-path record. It does not have a natural
addition that recreates occupancy, and it does not conflict with the
storage prohibition. Who records, when the enforcement point refuses
without classifying, is the remaining constructor question; it is
already answered in the ADR rather than left to the criterion's letter.

C7 is the one to read before it is built against. The others look
specified enough that implementation will teach less than C5 and C6
did — unless the `423` grows a URL.
