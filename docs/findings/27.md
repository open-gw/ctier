# 27 — C12 states coverage, not the generator

Spec **1.12.0**. Requires 25. Revised 19 September 2026 after 29,
35 and 36. Independent of 26's C1 restatement, which remains
stopped.

## Part 0 — 26's test, applied

What does a property-only restatement of C12 newly permit, and
does anything else close it?

**Request-time checking.** Permitted, and already the shape 11a
gave C10. Nothing else needed to close it. Fine.

**Vacuous satisfaction.** "No operation executes without an
assignment" is true of an implementation for which nothing is
ever unassigned — including one that never looks. C2 does not
close this: C2 assigns a known undeclared operation (default
Tier 4); it does not speak to a reachable request that matches
no assignment at all. C10's "a decision was made" can be
satisfied by a default execute. The hole is closed **inside
C12**: absence MUST NOT be permissive; a miss is refused and
**recorded** with the applied tier undetermined.

That is the opposite of 26. Dropping "recorded" from C1 had no
good answer because recording *was* the property. Dropping the
generator from C12 has a good answer because the generator was
only one mechanism, and the vacuous reading is forbidden by a
clause of C12 itself rather than by leaning on another
criterion.

The audit's two-of-ten mechanisms: **one of two.** C1 was
misclassified as a restatable mechanism (26). C12 was not.

## The recording half, and why it waited

35 showed a correct `503` with no record. A refusal nobody can
evidence is indistinguishable from a request that never happened.
Before 29, Levels 1 and 2 had no record sink, so requiring the
C12 miss to be recorded would have mandated something no
deployment below Level 3 could do. The clause is correct today
and would have been a defect on the 17th.

## Observations

C12 and C1 are both mechanism-shaped for the same historical
reason: they were written when the only implementation was a
generator and a policy service, so naming those was
indistinguishable from naming the property. Nothing was
careless. The defect became visible only when a second
architecture was contemplated — the strongest argument in this
project for building the second target early rather than as a
portability victory lap.

This task was drafted on 17 September with a recording clause no
deployment below Level 3 could then satisfy, and is correct on
the 19th because 29 and 35 landed in between. Spec text written
ahead of the implementation is not automatically wrong — 1.5.0
did it deliberately and declared the gap — but it is worth
knowing which of the two you are doing, and this one was
accidental.
